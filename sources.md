# burrow: evidence for connecting incoming subletters with the roommates who stay

Research checked: **November 20, 2026**. This brief recommends website claims; it does not change website content.

## Recommendation for the website

Use evidence about household friction and expectations, rather than broad housing-market or rent statistics. Lead with the problem, then explain how burrow gives the existing household a voice.

### Recommended stat cards

| Priority | Number | Suggested website copy | Citation beside the card |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — strongest college-specific headline | **24.2%** | Of responding undergraduates reported roommate or housemate problems in the past year. | [ACHA-NCHA, Spring 2026, question 47A8](https://www.acha.org/wp-content/uploads/NCHA-IIIb_SPRING_2026_UNDERGRADUATE_REFERENCE_GROUP_DATA_REPORT.pdf#page=49) |
| 2 — explains why habits matter | **60%** | Of surveyed US roommates selected cleanliness as a likely source of household tension. | [SpareRoom, August 2025 roommate census](https://www.spareroom.com/content/info-statistics/avoid-roommate-disputes) |
| 3 — consequence of a poor household fit | **8%** | Of surveyed US roommates said a roommate dispute made them leave their previous rental. | [SpareRoom, August 2025 roommate census](https://www.spareroom.com/content/info-statistics/avoid-roommate-disputes) |

If the page should emphasize everyday compatibility rather than moving out, replace the third card with **36% — Of surveyed US roommates selected noise as a likely source of tension.** Same SpareRoom source. These are separate findings, not percentages to add together.

Keep the population and date visible in a source footnote. Avoid shortening these to claims about all students, all renters, Ann Arbor residents, or subletters specifically.

### Suggested problem-to-solution copy

**Problem:** An incoming subletter may arrange the room with someone who is leaving, while knowing little about the roommates who will stay.

**Product response:** See the habits of the people you'll actually live with. Compare expectations, get every stayer's approval, and agree on household terms before taking the next step.

The product response describes burrow's existing workflow, not a research finding or a proven reduction in conflict. The external rationale is strongest in the university subletting guidance below: existing roommates should participate in choosing the incoming person. [University of Maryland guidance](https://och.umd.edu/resources/handouts/Uploads/HowtoSubleaseSublet.pdf)

## Source register and interpretation

### S1. American College Health Association — Spring 2026 undergraduate report

- **Primary source:** [ACHA-NCHA III Undergraduate Student Reference Group Data Report, Spring 2026](https://www.acha.org/wp-content/uploads/NCHA-IIIb_SPRING_2026_UNDERGRADUATE_REFERENCE_GROUP_DATA_REPORT.pdf).
- **Release:** August 26, 2026; [official report index](https://www.acha.org/ncha/data-results/survey-results/academic-year-2025-2026/).
- **Coverage:** 44,602 undergraduates at 86 US institutions. Question 47A8 has **44,320 valid responses**; **10,726 answered yes (24.2%)**. Locate it on PDF page 49, printed page 47.
- **Limitations:** Institutions opted into the survey. Mean institutional response proportion was 10%. This is a reference group, not a national probability estimate or a sample restricted to people currently sharing housing. The question covers problems/challenges, not specifically communication failures or sublet arrangements.
- **Use:** Best headline for the scale of the problem. It does not identify the cause or demonstrate an intervention's effectiveness.

### S2. SpareRoom — August 2025 US roommate census

- **Primary source:** [How to avoid roommate wars](https://www.spareroom.com/content/info-statistics/avoid-roommate-disputes).
- **Coverage:** 1,257 US roommates, including tenants and people sharing with their landlords; August 2025.
- **Findings:** Cleanliness **60%**; noise **36%**; leaving a previous rental because of a roommate dispute **8%**. The tension question permitted multiple selections.
- **Limitations:** Commercial survey; the page does not establish probability sampling or publish weighting/response-rate details. Not student-only or Ann Arbor-specific. Selecting a likely tension point is different from reporting an actual conflict.
- **Product interpretation:** Dishes, noise and sleep questions can surface expectations earlier; house terms can make expectations explicit. This is our design inference, not a measured effect of burrow.

### S3. Abela, Sharma & Law — undergraduate roommate study

- **Primary source:** [Abela, Sharma & Law, *Compromising, Avoiding, or Bullying?*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/21676968231216512), *Emerging Adulthood*; online November 17, 2023, journal issue 2024. DOI: [10.1177/21676968231216512](https://doi.org/10.1177/21676968231216512).
- **Coverage:** Reports N = 804, at one Southern Ontario university; data collected in winter/fall **2016**. See Methods and Descriptive Statistics.
- **Findings:** **52.8%** did not know their roommates before moving in and had not chosen them. **52.4%** reported misunderstandings among common disagreements.
- **Limitations:** Older, single-campus self-report data, largely first-year residence students. Item-specific denominators are not supplied with these percentages in the prose; do not describe them as percentages of all conflicts or of US subletters.
- **Use:** Supporting evidence only; not a current US sublet prevalence estimate.

### S4. University of Maryland — existing roommates' involvement in subletting

- **Primary source:** [Off-Campus Housing Services: How to Sublease/Sublet](https://och.umd.edu/resources/handouts/Uploads/HowtoSubleaseSublet.pdf), one-page handout; publication date not stated.
- **Guidance:** Include the current roommates in selecting a replacement and arrange for prospective incoming tenants to meet them. See the choosing-a-sublessee guidance and step 2 of the subletting process.
- **Use:** The closest direct match to burrow's stayer–incoming problem. It supports giving the people who remain input in the decision.
- **Limitations:** Practical university guidance, not a prevalence statistic, legal rule, or product evaluation. Do not turn it into a percentage of sublets that omit roommate consent.

### S5. University of Michigan — expectations and agreement before subletting

- **Primary source:** [Dean of Students / Beyond the Diag: Living On Your Own](https://deanofstudents.umich.edu/student-programs/beyond-the-diag/off-campus-living-guide/living-on-your-own/), Roommates section; publication date not stated.
- **Guidance:** Discuss quiet hours, guests, cleaning and summer plans before signing; make sure the unit's residents agree to a proposed sublet.
- **Use:** Local context for household approval and habit-based expectations. Present as guidance from U-M, not an endorsement of burrow or a claim about every lease's legal requirements.

## Claim boundaries

This search did not establish the rate at which Ann Arbor summer subletters fail to meet existing stayers. It also found no evaluation of burrow. The sources establish adjacent problems and recommended practices; they do not substantiate a numerical claim that this app prevents disputes, improves satisfaction, or reduces moves.

Avoid website claims such as “burrow reduces conflict by X%,” “60% of subletters fight about cleaning,” or “52.8% sign without meeting the stayers.” None follows from these findings.

To measure the app's effect, run a pilot that records, with consent:

1. Whether incoming tenants reviewed every stayer's habits and connected with the household before signing.
2. Whether all stayers had a say in the incoming selection.
3. Expectation clarity before move-in and reported mismatches 2–4 weeks later.

Use a comparison group before claiming an improvement attributable to burrow. For now, the credible website story is: **roommate problems are common, everyday expectations matter, and the people who stay deserve a voice.**
